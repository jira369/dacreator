# 3 directions dealt for this brief

Choose the one whose way of composing can best carry an idea from your inventory (SKILL.md steps 1 and 4), not the
most familiar one. Then deal it in full with the same command plus `--direction <id>` (and without --options).

## `dense-tiles` — The well-kept board
Idea engine: The whole page is a board of tiles fitted edge to edge with narrow gaps, like a tidy desk seen from above. Each tile is one thing: the name, a statement, a photograph, a number, a link, a short list.
Composition: A 12-column grid with 8px gaps and 8–12px outer margins, filled completely, no empty cells. Tiles use only four sizes (for example 6×4, 6×2, 3×2, 3×4 in column × row units of about 90px).

## `quiet-instrument` — The quiet instrument
Idea engine: Radical understatement. Where every competitor shouts, this page almost whispers: a headline barely larger than body text, a narrow column, small exact demonstrations, and a great deal of untouched space.
Composition: A single column 440–560px wide, placed left of centre (starting at about 22% of the viewport width) or dead centre, running the whole page. The headline is the one large thing on the first screen: two lines at display size (5–7vw), medium weight, starting on the column's left edge and allowed to run wider than the column.

## `one-motif` — One motif
Idea engine: Take one shape from the subject and let it run the whole system. A drilling tool's octagonal cross-section becomes the buttons, the image masks and the loader.
Composition: Hero: the motif appears once at enormous scale as a flat filled shape, cropped by the viewport (a circle 120vh across entering from the right, a chamfered block filling the lower half), with a short headline at 6–8vw and one supporting sentence set on the remaining ground, left-aligned on a shared margin. Through the page the motif returns as: the mask for every photograph (via `clip-path` or `border-radius` made from the same geometry), the shape of the primary button, the bullet beside list items at text size, and one full-width band that separates the two halves of the page.
